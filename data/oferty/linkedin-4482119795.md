The Mission of the Engineering Supply Chain Manager (Electronics)
As our
Engineering Supply Chain Manager (Electronics)
, you'll join our fast-moving Global Supply Chain organization and work closely with our hardware engineering teams as they design and build electronic systems on tight and shifting timelines.
You'll be an active part of our engineering projects, helping to execute them and staying ahead of supply. That means identifying long lead items and critical components as early as possible in the process, and securing them before they become bottlenecks for testing and builds. When lead times, allocation or a late design change threaten a prototype schedule, we need you to act. Our development cycles are fast and iterations are constantly occurring since we thrive on pushing the technology for the best.
You'll also be a key link between Engineering and Production. You'll communicate R&D supply chain risks and the critical components you've identified to Production, so nothing comes as a surprise at handover.
Your work supports the success of every engineering prototype iteration and at the same time prepares Production for rollout and mass production.
This role suits someone who is proactive by nature, with a solution driven mindset and a deep understanding of electronics, PCB manufacturing and hardware prototyping, who is comfortable with ambiguity and acts before problems arrive rather than waiting for them. How quickly we get from concept to working hardware in orbit, and then into production, depends on how well this role is done.

Your Day-to-day Responsibilities
High-Velocity Engineering Support
Serve as the hands-on supply chain counterpart for fast-paced electronics engineering teams working on multiple, rapidly evolving hardware projects.
Be an active member of engineering project teams from kick-off, helping to execute the project plan and reviewing draft BOMs while the design can still be changed.
Identify long lead items and critical components as early as possible, and secure them proactively (early orders, allocation, buffer stock) before they can delay a project.
Translate early-stage concepts, evolving BOMs, and mid-design changes into actionable sourcing steps with minimal delay.
Advise engineers, in real time and in early design reviews, on how component choices affect availability, alternates, obsolescence, cost and lead time.
Identify supply issues before they become blockers and propose workaround paths immediately.
Rapid Sourcing & Procurement Execution
Own sourcing and procurement of electronic components, PCB fabrication, PCBA assembly, connectors, harness, sensors, RF parts, embedded modules.
Run fast quoting and supplier selection so prototype and builds get their boards and parts on the dates engineering committed to.
Lead efforts to find alternative components or new supply paths within hours, not weeks, when shortages occur.
Set stock levels and material availability plans for development and prototype builds.
Supplier Interaction & Technical Engagement
Own relationships with PCB fabricators, electronics manufacturing partners and component distributors, and work with them to understand lead time constraints, manufacturing capabilities, DFM implications and fast-track build options.
Support technical capability assessments of PCB/PCBA vendors, machining partners, and component distributors.
Negotiate priority build slots, allocation and expedited shipments, and secure prototypes under compressed timelines.
Cross-Functional Start-Up Collaboration
Coordinate across engineering, manufacturing, program management, and operations to remove obstacles and maintain build readiness.
Maintain complete visibility into prototype schedules, engineering changes, and material risks, communicating clearly and frequently.
Operate with a bias for action: if something is unclear, you chase it; if something breaks, you fix it; if something is missing, you find it.
Agility, Risk Management & Continuous Improvement
Build and maintain a living view of supply risks across all active prjects, including obsolescence, allocation, single-source parts, counterfeit risk and export restrictions and qualify alternatives early.
Keep purchasing accurate in the ERP system, with clean traceability, cost visibility and build readiness status that the organisation can rely on, even under fast-moving conditions.
Bring an experimentation mindset: test new suppliers, new sourcing channels, new manufacturing partners, and new tools to reduce lead times and cost.
R&D to Production Readiness
Communicate R&D supply chain risks and identified critical components to Production early and clearly, so they are known well before handover.
Support the success and rapid iteration of engineering prototypes while preparing Production for rollout and mass production.
Make sure critical and long lead parts, qualified suppliers and realistic lead times and capacity are in place before a design moves into series production.
Help build the sourcing foundations that will carry a design from prototype into repeatable production.
